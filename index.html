    How to read the field
    • Each dot is one man. Blue: Athenians. Red: Boeotians.
    • The bronze arc is his shield; the line is his spear, which jabs when he strikes.
    • Bronze dot: veteran. Bronze ring: the unit's leader.
    • Darker dot: wounded. Dashed ring: wavering. Faded: running away. ×: dead.
    • Small dots on the ground are empty places in the formation.
    • "Show mood" rings men whose nerve is going in orange, and puts a dark wedge in front of men straining to get at the enemy.
    • A man stepping back from an attacker behind his shield is refusing the fight: tired men do it if there is room behind them, and a disciplined unit can give ground this way as a whole.
    • Cohesion is how well the men keep their places. Running loses it; standing still restores it.
    • Front-rank men lean forward as they work up the resolve to step into the gap. Eager men behind press up and close the ranks; when the word to step back reaches them, they ease off.
