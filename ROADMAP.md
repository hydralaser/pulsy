# Pulsy — what is planned for later

Ideas agreed or proposed along the way and not yet built, so none of them gets lost. Roughly in
the order they are likely to come. DESIGN.md describes what is built.

## The village (campaign layer)

### Raids (stage 2, once the village is fully fleshed out)
- Raiders come over the hills from Boeotia by the north track. A lookout or a watch on the border
  road gives some warning; without one, less.
- Whoever is out in the fields, the woods or on the pasture is caught outside the walls and has to
  run for the gate.
- The old Greek choice: go out and fight to save the crops and the flock, or stay behind the wall
  and watch them burn the fields and drive off the sheep.
- The fight is the battle model, with the village's own men (their courage, skill, standing and the
  kit their households own) on the village's own ground. Wounds, deaths and marks come home with
  them; a man lamed in the fight farms slower and may no longer stand in the front rank.
- Losses to the village: burned crops, sheaves on the threshing floor, stolen sheep, a burned
  woodpile at a landing.

### Growth (stage 3)
- **The smith:** makes and mends kit. A household's wealth, and the smith being alive, decide who
  can afford bronze.
- **Wealth and kit:** households buy their sons a panoply as they come of age; a rich household's
  son arrives with a cuirass.
- **Bonds between people:** men who work, eat and fight together build ties. In battle a man holds
  better beside his brother-in-law or his work-mate and breaks harder when that man falls; the
  player places friends together in the line. Debt between households sours ties.
- **What goodwill and ties do** (they are kept, but do nothing yet): households with little goodwill
  obey orders grudgingly or not at all, petition rudely, lend to you unwillingly, or leave the
  village; ties between households decide who stands beside whom in the battle line and who will
  lend to whom; a house you have favoured repays it.
- **More kinds of petition:** to marry a daughter into a particular house, for relief from the
  common granary, for help with a debt, to settle a quarrel between houses (you judge it).
- **Healing:** wounds heal over weeks; lasting marks stay and affect work as well as war.
- **More building:** wall repair; a common granary building; a smithy; a watchtower on the border
  road; bigger houses for rich households. (Houses for new households are built.)
- **Household life, further:** widows remarrying, adoption of an heir by a house without sons
  (common in Athens), daughters inheriting as epikleroi, slaves bought and freed.
- **Patronage and debt politics:** households deep in debt to a rich one become its dependants;
  cancelling debts (as Solon did) as a choice for the leading man.

### Going raiding (stage 4)
- Taking a band out for bronze, cattle and glory, leaving the village thinner while they are gone.
- Resources the village cannot get at home (bronze, iron) by raid or by trade.

### The economy and land, later
- **Traders:** pedlars and traders at the gate who buy cloth, wool, cheese and timber for bronze,
  iron, salt and coin.
- **Olives and vines:** the olive harvest in winter and the vintage in autumn, for food, oil and
  trade.
- **Threshing as its own step:** sheaves carried to the threshing floor and threshed by oxen in
  summer, so a raid can catch the harvest on the floor.
- **Pasture limits:** overgrazing; the flock grazing the stubble after harvest; summer pasture in
  the hills.
- **Animals:** fodder for the oxen in winter, donkeys for carrying, more yokes and carts as
  households can afford them.
- **Festivals and rest days,** sickness that spreads, the dead buried with rites.

### A wider war (only if the village makes it seem worth it)
- A calendar of years and campaign seasons in which outcomes change what comes next: a defeat
  brings an invasion, a victory leaves the enemy fewer veterans.
- A strategic map.
- Optionally a chain of historical battles (Delion 424, Mantinea 418...) rather than generated ones.

## The battle

- Scars and marks carried from one battle to the next once the village links them.
- Veterans persisting between battles, with their records.
